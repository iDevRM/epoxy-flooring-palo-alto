What Does Garage Floor Epoxy Involve?

Garage floor epoxy is a layered system, and each layer has a job. The floor is ground, repaired, coated, covered in flakes and sealed, usually over one to two days.

  • Diamond grinding to remove old paint and open the concrete
  • Crack and chip repair, ground flush with the slab
  • A pigmented base coat rolled out in sections
  • A full broadcast of color flakes into the wet base coat
  • Scraping and vacuuming loose flakes once the base cures
  • A clear, UV-stable topcoat that seals the flakes in

You'll need to move cars, shelving and loose items out before the crew arrives. Wall-mounted cabinets and EV chargers can stay where they are.

When Do You Need a New Garage Floor?

A garage floor coating is worth looking at when the slab starts causing everyday problems. These are the signs that bring most homeowners to call:

  • Paint or a DIY kit peeling in patches where the tires park
  • Oil, coolant and rust stains that won't scrub out
  • Gray dust that tracks into the house on shoes
  • Hairline cracks and chipped control joints
  • Plans to turn the garage into a gym, office or workshop
  • A new EV charger and a garage clean-out already underway

Why Do Garage Floor Coatings Peel?

Garage floor coatings peel for three main reasons, and none of them are bad luck. The first is skipped prep: a coating rolled over unground or acid-etched concrete has very little to hold onto.

The second is moisture. Water vapor rising through the slab pushes against the coating from below, which is more common on older slabs without a vapor barrier and on clay soils closer to the Bay. A moisture test before coating catches this.

The third is hot tire pickup. Thin store-bought paints soften under warm tires, and when the car pulls out the coating lifts with the tread. A thick, fully cured system over a ground slab doesn't have this problem.

Flake Epoxy vs. Solid Color: Which Do You Need?

Both finishes go over the same prep and use a clear topcoat. The difference is how the floor looks and how it handles daily life.

Flake floorSolid color floor
LookSpeckled, granite-like blendClean, single color with a gloss finish
Hides dirt and wearVery well — dust and scuffs blend inShows dust, tire marks and scratches more
TextureLight natural texture from the flakesSmooth unless anti-slip grit is added
Best forParking garages, workshops, busy family garagesShowroom-style garages, gyms, display spaces

Most garages that see daily parking end up with a flake floor because it's forgiving. If you want a cleaner look, solid color with a touch of grit works well in a gym or display garage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to empty the garage before the install?

Yes, the floor has to be clear so it can be ground edge to edge. Cars, shelving and boxes need to come out, but wall-mounted cabinets and EV chargers can usually stay in place.

Will the flakes hide the cracks in my garage floor?

Flakes hide small surface marks well, but cracks are filled and ground flush first so they don't show through. Moving cracks can still reappear as a fine line over time, and we point those out at the estimate.

Will hot tires lift the new coating?

No. Hot tire pickup happens with thin paints and DIY kits that soften under warm rubber. A professional system over a ground slab is thick and fully cured, so tires don't pull it up.

Can the coating go up the garage stem wall?

Yes. Wrapping the coating a few inches up the stem wall gives the garage a finished edge and protects the bottom of the wall from water and scuffs. It's listed as an option on the written quote.
