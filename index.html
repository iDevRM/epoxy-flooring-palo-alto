What Is Epoxy Flooring in Palo Alto?

Epoxy flooring is a two-part resin coating that bonds to bare concrete and hardens into a tough, seamless surface. In Palo Alto, most of these floors go into garages, but they also show up in home gyms, workshops, labs and restaurant back rooms.

The resin is mixed on site and spread over a slab that has been diamond-ground. Grinding opens up the top of the concrete so the coating can soak in and lock on. A clear topcoat goes on last and takes the daily wear from tires, tools and foot traffic.

Homeowners usually call when the garage floor is dusty, stained or covered in peeling paint. Business owners call when a floor needs to stand up to carts, chemicals or hot-water cleaning.

When Do You Need Epoxy Flooring?

Most calls start with one of these situations.

Old paint is peeling where the tires sit

Garage floor paint and DIY kits often lift under hot tires within a year or two. Once the edges start curling, the peeling keeps spreading.

The slab sheds dust and soaks up stains

Bare concrete gives off fine gray dust and absorbs oil, coolant and paint. A sealed floor wipes clean with a damp mop.

You're adding an EV charger

Putting in a charger is a natural time to clear the garage, fix the floor and make the space easier to keep clean.

The garage is becoming a gym, studio or office

A bright, sealed floor makes a converted garage feel like part of the house instead of overflow storage.

You're getting ready to sell

A finished garage floor stands out when buyers tour the home, and it is one of the quicker upgrades to finish before a listing goes live.

A business floor is wearing through

Rolling carts, spills and daily cleaning grind bare concrete down. A commercial coating adds a wear layer that's easy to mark and wash.

How Epoxy Flooring Works in Palo Alto

A typical two-car garage takes one to two days on site. Here's what happens from the first call to parking on the new floor.

  1. Free on-site estimate

    We measure the floor, look at every crack, test the slab for moisture and ask how you use the space. You get a written quote before anything is scheduled.

  2. Diamond grinding

    A walk-behind grinder with a vacuum shroud removes old paint, sealer and stains and opens up the surface. The dust goes into the vacuum, not into your house.

  3. Crack and chip repair

    Cracks, pits and chipped joints are filled with a fast-setting repair material and ground flush with the floor.

  4. Base coat and broadcast

    The base coat goes down, followed by color flakes, a solid tint or metallic pigment.

  5. Clear topcoat

    A UV-stable topcoat seals everything in. Fine anti-slip grit can be mixed in for areas that get wet.

  6. Cure and walk-through

    Foot traffic the next day and cars back in after a few days. We walk the finished floor with you before we leave.

A local detail: winter mornings here can be cold and damp, and resin cures slowly on a chilly slab. Between November and March we check slab temperature and dew point before coating and plan the schedule around the weather.

Epoxy or Polyaspartic: Which Fits Your Palo Alto Home?

Homeowners here often ask whether they should pick epoxy or a polyaspartic system. Both bond to ground concrete and both look great. The real differences are cure time, UV resistance and how the floor will be used.

Epoxy is thick and builds up well over rough or uneven concrete. It is a strong choice for interior garages, shops and basements. It cures slowly, so the garage is usually out of use for a few days, and standard epoxy can yellow in direct sun.

Polyaspartic cures much faster, often allowing foot traffic the same day. It stays clear and color-stable in sunlight, which matters for garages that sit open on sunny afternoons in Midtown or Barron Park. A polyaspartic floor coating is also flexible, so it handles temperature swings without cracking.

Many of our installs combine the two: an epoxy base for build and bond, topped with a polyaspartic clear coat for UV and scratch resistance. That mix gives you the strengths of both.

Not sure which one you need? We test your slab, ask how you use the space, and recommend the system that makes sense. No pressure, just a straight answer.

Living With a Coated Floor on the Peninsula

Mild weather makes Palo Alto one of the easiest places to own a coated floor. A few habits keep it looking new.

Palo Alto garages rarely see the hot-tire damage that ruins floors in hotter inland cities, and winters are too mild for road salt. What they do see is redwood debris, bike grease, wet leaves tracked in from Foothills trails and the fine grit that drifts in off El Camino Real. None of it is a threat to the finish, but all of it dulls the shine if it sits for weeks.

Sweep or dust-mop every week or two, and rinse the tire paths after a rainy drive. For a deeper clean, warm water with a little neutral cleaner is all a concrete coating needs. Skip citrus degreasers and anything acidic, which can haze a topcoat over time. Wipe up motor oil and brake fluid the same day, not because they will stain a sealed floor but because they are slippery.

Put felt or rubber pads under heavy items that stay in one place, such as a tool chest or a kayak rack, and use a mat under the e-bike charger to catch drips. If a worn patch shows up where you park after many years, that is the topcoat doing its job. A fresh clear coat over the existing system restores the gloss without starting over, and we can usually do that in a single morning.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does epoxy flooring cost in Palo Alto?

The price depends on the size of the floor, how much repair the concrete needs and which coating system you pick. We measure and test the slab at a free on-site estimate and give you a written quote before anything is booked.

Can you put epoxy on an Eichler floor with radiant heat?

Yes, in most cases. A coating only needs the very top of the slab ground, which stays well above the heating tubes in a typical radiant floor. What we never do on a radiant slab is saw-cut or drill without knowing where the tubes run, so cracks are filled and ground flush instead.

How long does an epoxy garage floor last?

A professionally installed coating over a properly ground slab usually lasts 15 to 20 years in a home garage. The clear topcoat takes most of the wear, and it can be scuffed and recoated later instead of redoing the whole floor.

How soon can I park on a new epoxy floor?

Most floors take foot traffic the next day, and cars can usually go back in after a few days. Cure time depends on the coating system and the temperature, and you get the exact timeline for your floor at the estimate.

Can you coat a garage floor that has an EV charger?

Yes. The charger and its conduit stay in place, and the coating is cut in neatly around any mount or pedestal. If a charger is coming soon, it helps to have the electrical work done first so nothing gets drilled through the new floor.

Is an epoxy floor slippery?

A smooth gloss finish can get slick when it is wet. Flake floors have a natural texture, and fine anti-slip grit can be added to the topcoat for garages, patios and commercial floors.

Do you have to grind the concrete before coating it?

Yes. Diamond grinding opens the surface so the resin bonds into the concrete instead of sitting on top of it. Acid etching alone is a weaker substitute and one of the most common reasons garage coatings peel.
